The machine drafts are being finalized.

We are also refining the contribution interface so readers can propose and review corrections one line at a time. Everything here is preliminary: the machine-generated English is a working aid, not a finished translation or scholarly edition. Human review is the next stage.

Improve one line

You do not need to understand the whole editorial system. If a draft line is unclear or wrong, fix that line.

  1. 1. Read

    Compare the original-language transcription with the machine draft.

  2. 2. Improve

    Choose “Improve translation,” sign in with GitHub, and write better English. A note is optional.

  3. 3. Review

    Your suggestion becomes public immediately as a proposal. Editors decide what enters the checked translation.

What the labels mean

The site does not disguise machine output as finished scholarship.

Machine draft
Generated English awaiting human correction. Useful for reading, but not yet checked.
Pending approval
An editor has flagged a published machine draft for focused correction before human signoff.
Human checked
A person has reviewed the English. The public proposal history still shows how it changed.

Sources stay visible

Open Dead Sea began with a Cohere-supported machine-translation project. The site keeps generated English, source transcription, and human judgment visibly separate.

  • Transcription

    Hebrew and Aramaic wording comes from ETCBC/dss and is credited on every manuscript.

  • Photographs

    We link to the Israel Antiquities Authority and holding libraries instead of copying their images.

  • Changes

    Suggestions and editorial decisions remain public so readers can inspect the work.
